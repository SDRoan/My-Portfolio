**WebP.** `scripts/pack-assets.mjs --images` re-encodes a texture as WebP
(cwebp `-q 88 -m 6`) only where that saves at least 20% and the result stays
at or above 40 dB PSNR against the source. These images were converted:
Earth's day, night and cloud maps, and the Iapetus, Io, Mars, Phobos, Titan and
Triton mosaics. The Moon, Mercury, Enceladus and other greyscale mosaics, and
the Earth region images, didn't qualify and stay JPEG. AVIF and KTX2 were not
used: no encoder for them is available in the build environment.

## Elevation (`space/data/surfaces/`)

Each grid is stored as int16 heights in metres, losslessly encoded as
"H2D1" (`space/engine/packs.js`): planar prediction (west + north − northwest),
with the residuals split into low-byte and high-byte planes, then gzipped.
This is 8–20% smaller than the earlier row-delta encoding, which the decoder
still reads. Rows run from
north to south, and columns from longitude −180° eastward. The browser
decodes them itself. A `.json` file beside each grid records its size, range,
source and licence.

**Correction: Mercury orientation.** An earlier version said the Solar System
Scope Mercury map was centred on 180° and shifted it half a turn. That was
wrong. The map was fine; the USGS MESSENGER DEM is the product centred on 180°
(its GeoTIFF GeoKeys give a central meridian of 180°), so the shift had
rotated the correct map to match the misread DEM. Every source is now read
with its own central meridian. Twelve USGS products are centred on 180°: the
Mercury, Ceres and Pluto DEMs, the Enceladus shape grid, and the Ceres,
Pluto, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto, Titan, Enceladus and Rhea mosaics. Each is
rotated at build time so column 0 is −180°. `scripts/planetary/validate-alignment.mjs`
checks that each map's fine detail correlates with its DEM at 0° offset, and
records every source's georeferencing in `space/data/surfaces/alignment-report.txt`.

### Mars high-resolution packs

Each pack is a lazy patch: it loads only within reach of its window. It is
resampled from the published DTM and feathered into the HRSC–MOLA 200 m
surface with a chamfer-distance blend. The pack's median offset from that
surface is subtracted, so both sit on the same datum (sphere 3396.19 km).
Gaps in the source are filled from HRSC–MOLA.

| Pack | Source (USGS Astrogeology) | Window | Grid | Size | Datum offset removed | Source coverage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `gale-hirise` | MSL Gale HiRISE DEM mosaic 1 m v3 (`MSL_Gale_DEM_Mosaic_1m_v3.tif`) | −5.10…−4.13° N, 137.12…137.68° E | 25 m (every 25th cell), 1319 × 2297 | 1.69 MB | −11.3 m | 82% |
| `jezero-ctx` | Mars 2020 landing-site CTX DTM 20 m (`J03_045994_1986_J03_046060_1986_20m_DTM.tif`) | 17.95…18.9° N, 77.08…77.80° E | 20 m, 2093 × 2789 | 1.85 MB | +3.5 m | 69% |
| `valles-hrsc`, `noctis-hrsc`, `elysium-hrsc` | HRSC–MOLA blend 200 m, every 2nd cell | the landmark windows | 399 m | 2.92 / 1.23 / 0.62 MB | — | — |

No public DTM finer than HRSC–MOLA covers Olympus Mons, Valles Marineris,
Noctis Labyrinthus or Elysium Mons as a whole, so those stay at 200–400 m.

The Thomas files use west longitude; they are converted to east-positive
longitude. This was checked against the HRSC Phobos DEM (r = 0.967 with the
conversion, 0.873 without). Makemake, Eris and the Uranian moons keep spheres
or catalogue ellipsoids: no shape model with relief is published for them.

**Data-constrained reconstructions.** These bodies have no DEM, so the
terrain is built from measured positions and sizes and published
morphometry. In the simulator it is labelled "RECONSTRUCTED FEATURES",
never "real DEM". `scripts/planetary/build-features.mjs` writes 989 features
to `space/data/surfaces/features.json` (112 KB):

- **Craters:** positions and diameters from the IAU Gazetteer for Mimas,
  Tethys, Dione, Rhea, Iapetus, Ganymede, Callisto, Europa, Titan, Miranda,
  Ariel, Umbriel, Titania, Oberon and Triton. Depth, rim and central-peak
  profiles come from the simple-to-complex scaling with surface gravity, not
  from measured depths.
- **Io:** paterae and montes at Gazetteer positions and sizes. **Titan:**
  maria and lacus as level seas. **Miranda:** coronae as raised outlines.
- **Named measured features:**
  - Herschel on Mimas: 139 km across, about 10 km deep, with a rim of about
    2.5 km and a central peak of about 6 km (Moore et al. 2004, *Icarus* 171).
  - The Iapetus equatorial ridge along Toledo Montes, 180.7–267.3° E: up to
    13 km high, with a 65 km base (Giese et al. 2008, *Icarus* 193).
  - The four Enceladus tiger stripes (Alexandria, Cairo, Baghdad, Damascus):
    troughs about 500 m deep and 2 km wide, with 100 m flanking ridges (Porco
    et al. 2006, *Science* 311).
  - Verona Rupes on Miranda, about 5 km high in the model. The direction its
    cliff faces is not verified.
- **Surface character** (procedural, held to the published relief):
  - Europa: low ridges and lineae, with chaos texture.
  - Io: plains, with no crater field.
  - Titan: dunes and low relief.
  - Triton: cantaloupe-terrain texture.

Landmark coordinates for these bodies come from the IAU/USGS Gazetteer of
Planetary Nomenclature data files (`asc-planetarynames-data`, `*_nomenclature_center_pts`).

**Building heights.** Heights come from the OSM `height` tag where present (feet
converted to metres). Otherwise they are `building:levels` × 3 m (+ `roof:levels`).
Otherwise they are a conservative fixed estimate from the building type (house
7 m, apartments 14 m, warehouse 9 m, …) or footprint area (3.5–10 m). Every
building records which rule set its height. Per region (tagged / from levels /
estimated):

| Region | Tagged | From levels | Estimated |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Building outlines that have `building:part`s are drawn as their parts, which
is the OSM 3D-building convention. Facades are procedural: window rhythm by
building type, with colour varied by a hash of the location. They are not
photographs.

**Overture Maps** was evaluated but not used. Reading it needs scanning the
global GeoParquet release; in these regions its buildings largely derive from
OSM, which is used directly.

**Peaks.** Kilimanjaro, Fuji, Denali, Aconcagua, Mont Blanc, the Matterhorn
and K2 each get a pack holding only Copernicus GLO-30 elevation (≈ 30 m),
so each summit is the real terrain.

**The landmark registry** (`space/data/earth/landmarks.json`) is built by
`scripts/earth/build-landmarks.mjs`. It takes a list of Wikidata ids and
fills in the rest from two independent sources:

- [Wikidata](https://www.wikidata.org/) (CC0): coordinates, published height
  and elevation (normalized to SI units), type and country.
- [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) (ODbL): the same
  feature, fetched from the official API by the OSM ids Wikidata lists.

The distance between the two sources is recorded, and disagreements are
reported, never averaged. Inside a detailed pack, the landmark's footprint
and drawn height are looked up too. The info panel shows each landmark's
**source accuracy** class.

`scripts/earth/validate-world.mjs` checks the result against independent
sources:

- landmarks: Wikidata against OSM
- peaks: the DEM summit against Wikidata elevations
- airports: drawn runways against [OurAirports](https://ourairports.com/data/)
  thresholds (public domain)
- rivers: drawn water against Natural Earth centrelines

Its report is `space/data/earth/world-validation.txt`.

Not used, with reasons:

- **London and Heathrow:** no redistributable national orthophoto; Sentinel-2 only.
- **Sydney:** the NSW Spatial Services imagery licence is published inconsistently (CC BY 4.0 in one place, CC BY-ND 4.0 in another); Sentinel-2 only.

**NAIP extent fix.** The NAIP ImageServer serves square pixels in degrees and
silently widens any other requested box. The NYC, Kennedy, Grand Canyon and
Rainier NAIP layers had been registered with the requested box, not the
served one, which compressed them north–south by up to ~30%. Their bounds are
now the served extent, and both NAIP fetchers request square-degree sizes.

**Colour matching.** Each fine orthophoto (IGN, GSI, PDOK, NAIP) is scaled
per channel, by a gain only, so its mean matches the Sentinel-2 layer over
the same area. This keeps the finer box from showing as a differently tinted
rectangle (`scripts/earth/match-imagery.py`). It carries over the Sentinel-2
scene's season. Black no-data pixels (NAIP beyond the coast) are not draped.

- WARN — PARTIAL DATA

## Global Earth base layer (`space/data/earth/global/`)

This is what every place on Earth has, inside the region packs or not. It is
built by `scripts/earth/fetch-global-landcover.mjs` and
`scripts/earth/build-global.mjs`. The output is 184 bundles of 15° × 15°,
22.5 MB in total.

| Layer | Source | Licence / attribution | Processing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Land cover | [ESA WorldCover 10 m 2021 v200](https://esa-worldcover.org/), all 2,651 tiles. Only each COG's internal level-5 overview (≈ 300 m) is range-read, about 0.4% of the 124 GB product | CC BY 4.0. "© ESA WorldCover project 2021 / Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data (2021) processed by ESA WorldCover consortium", shown on screen near the ground | Aggregated to 1/120° cells (≈ 925 m): the dominant class, then a 3 × 3 majority filter, plus the cell's water share in quarters. Stored as lossless WebP, 18.8 MB |
| Major roads | [Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/) 10 m roads v5.1 | Public domain | Ferries dropped; simplified to ~40 m; delta-encoded |
| Rivers | Natural Earth 10 m rivers and lake centrelines, Europe and North America supplements | Public domain | Lake centrelines dropped (lakes come from the land cover). Width estimated from the feature's rank (40–500 m) |

**What it draws:**

- **Ground colour and texture.** The terrain shader colours the ground near
  the camera by land-cover class: canopy for forest, a patchwork of fields for
  cropland, pavement for built-up land, and so on. It draws shorelines, lakes
  and wide rivers from the water share. From orbit the Blue Marble map still
  shows.
- **Vegetation.** Trees, shrubs and grass are generated by the same code as
  in the packs. Density is set by class (closed canopy in tree cover,
  scattered trees in open land). Families are broad categories by climate
  zone (latitude and dryness), never species.
- **Major roads and rivers** are drawn as 3D ribbons, rivers as water.
- **Urban fallback.** In built-up land cover, procedural streets and
  rectangular buildings are laid out: one grid orientation per 0.05°
  district, with density and height set by how built-up the surroundings
  are. **These are not real buildings or streets.** They carry height source
  "procedural (global urban fallback, not mapped)", and the surface panel and
  on-screen credit say so.

Antarctica (south of 60° S, where WorldCover has no tiles) is treated as
snow and ice. Open ocean is water.

**Exact versus generated.** Each OSM tree stands at its mapped position. It
uses its mapped size when tagged, and otherwise a conservative mid-range size
for its category. Its leaf type or genus picks the visual family (needle-leaved
→ conifer, palm genera → palm, mangrove genera → mangrove). No species is ever
shown unless its tags name one. Tree rows place trees along the mapped line,
and orchards and vineyards are planted in regular rows along each field's
long side.

Everything else is **generated** from the land-cover class and canopy density
on a deterministic jittered lattice (blue noise). It is kept off buildings,
roads, paths, rail, runways (with a 30 m clear zone), lakes, tidal water,
mapped parking, aprons and piers, and the launch site. It is thinned on steep
ground, and never placed on snow, ice, water or bare rock. Generated plants
use broad visual families chosen per region and elevation band; they are not
species.

**What the families are based on.** The regional family mixes and elevation
bands follow published vegetation zones, and land cover still decides whether
anything grows:

- Kennedy: pine flatwoods, oak hammock and cabbage palm.
- Grand Canyon: pinyon–juniper woodland below the ponderosa-pine rim forest
  (NPS).
- Mount Rainier: montane to subalpine forest.
- Khumbu: lower forest giving way to shrub.

Tree models, shaders and wind are original procedural work, with no external
models or textures.

## Packaging

Built by `scripts/pack-assets.mjs`, which every build script calls at the end:

- Planetary 15° tiles are packed 2 × 2 into `tiles/<body>/b<row>_<col>.bin`.
- Earth map tiles are packed 5 × 5 into `regions/<id>/t/<row>_<col>.bin`.

Both use the "SXB1" container: an index of keys, offsets and lengths,
followed by the gzip members as they are. This cut the files from 2,028 to
518 for the planetary tiles and from 3,324 to 289 for the region tiles.
`scripts/report-assets.mjs` lists the largest files, duplicates and
unreferenced files.

## Reference values used in code

- **Landmark coordinates** (`space/nav/targets.js`): the
  [IAU Gazetteer of Planetary Nomenclature](https://planetarynames.wr.usgs.gov/)
  for the Moon and Mars, USGS GNIS and national surveys for Earth, as cited
  on Wikipedia. Each was checked against the elevation grids; Olympus Mons,
  for example, reads +19.8 km and Hellas −6.1 km.
- **Mars dust** (`space/render/bodies.js`): a vertical optical depth of 0.5
  is typical of the clear season (Lemmon et al. 2015,
  [Icarus 251](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icarus.2014.03.029)). The colour is
  an effective albedo, more saturated than the measured single-scattering
  albedo, because the model has no multiple scattering. It's an
  approximation.
- **Reference shapes:** WGS 84 for Earth (a = 6378.137 km, f = 1/298.257).
  The other radii, flattenings and masses come from JPL, as listed in
  `data/astronomy/solar-system.json`.

## Worldwide cells, the free-only audit and hosting

**Worldwide cells.** `scripts/earth/world-cells.json` lists places built by
the same pipeline as every Overture pack: Overture buildings, roads, water,
land use and infrastructure; Copernicus GLO-30 elevation; Sentinel-2 10 m
imagery; ESA WorldCover vegetation. Adding a cell is one line. The 12
prototypes are:

- **Cities:** Berlin, Chattogram, São Paulo, Nairobi, Seoul, Mexico City and
  Auckland.
- **Rural:** Beauce (France), Tefé (Amazonas), villages near Lilongwe
  (Malawi), Gopalganj (Bangladesh) and Wagga Wagga (Australia).

The licences and attribution are those listed above for the city packs.

**Free-only policy.** `space/data/licenses.json` lists every dataset,
library, asset and service with its licence, cost, account and billing
requirements. `node scripts/validate-free-resources.mjs` fails on anything
that is:

- paid, a trial, or billing-required
- of a licence not on the allow-list
- from a forbidden proprietary provider (Google Maps, Earth or 3D Tiles;
  Bing; Mapbox services; Cesium ion; Esri basemaps; OSM tile servers)
- carrying an API key or token (NASA's public `DEMO_KEY` excepted)
- loading from an external runtime host not declared in the manifest

At runtime, visitors' browsers request only:

- the site's own files
- NASA's free NeoWs feed (homepage near-Earth-object view only, with a
  shipped fallback)

**Browser cache.** Map bundles and elevation grids go through a bounded
Cache Storage layer (`space/engine/datacache.js`):

- **Size:** 300 MB on desktop, 150 MB on phones.
- **Eviction:** least-recently-used first.
- **Versioning:** keyed to the site build, so a new build drops the old
  cache.
- **Fallback:** where Cache Storage is unavailable, data comes straight from
  the network.

**Hosting (free):**

- The app and the packs are served by GitHub Pages from the public
  repository. GitHub's documented limits are a published site of up to 1 GB
  and a soft 100 GB/month of bandwidth. Pages responses carry
  `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`.
- GitHub Release assets are not used for runtime data: their downloads send
  no CORS header, so a browser can't read them.
- Exact worldwide data (~1 TB, see the README) can't be hosted for free, so
  Earth is tiered: a global base layer everywhere plus detailed cells within
  the free budget.
